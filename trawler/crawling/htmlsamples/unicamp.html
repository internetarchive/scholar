Abstract: Vulvodynia is a prevalent chronic vulvar pain which has strong negative effects over quality of life of women and their partners. Pelvic floor muscles (PFM) hypertonicity is known to play an important role in its pathophysiology. For many years, studies investigating whether physical therapy was efficient on changing muscle status of PFM of women with vulvodynia used digital and electromyography assessments, with inconsistent findings. The four-dimensional translabial ultrasound (4D-TLUS) is proven to be efficient on measuring biometry of PFM. Recent studies found that biometric measures of PFM of women with vulvodynia differ from those of healthy controls. However, there is a lack of scientific evidence of whether physical therapy is effective on promoting changes on PFM of women with vulvodynia. This knowledge would contribute to improve knowledge about the mechanism throughout physical therapy is effective for treating women with vulvodynia, and to guide future studies aiming to increase effect of treatment for this disabling condition. To evaluate biometric changes of pelvic floor muscles of women diagnosed with vulvodynia submitted to physical therapy, using 4D-TLUS. This single blinded randomized controlled trial assessed pelvic floor muscles of 86 women with vulvodynia, using 4D-TLUS. The imaging was obtained at rest, maximum voluntary contraction and valsalva maneuver after bladder was empty. Axial and midsagittal planes were analysed using 4D-View software, where biometric measures of PFM (levator plate angle, anorectal angle, antero-posterior distance, area of puborectal muscles, left-right and antero-posterior diameters) were measured. In addition, patients answered to the Female Sexual Function Index (FSFI) questionnaire and reported the mean of sexual pain experienced on last 30 days, based on numerical rate scale (NRS) from 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst pain). Volunteers were randomized either into oral medication with tricyclic antidepressant (TCA) or both TCA and a protocol of kinesiotherapy including contraction and stretching exercises for the PFM. Outcomes were assessed at baseline and after 8 weeks of the treatment. Both groups had significant improvement for sexual function and pain (p>0.05). Compared to baseline assessment, women with vulvodynia submitted to TCA and kinesiotherapy had significant increase on antero-posterior diameters at rest (p=.008) and at contraction (p=.004), larger levator hiatus area and antero-posterior diameter at contraction, and left-right diameter at valsalva (p values<0.05). Control group treated with only TCA did not have significant changes from baseline when assessed after eight weeks (p>.05). Comparison between groups showed that volunteers had levator plate angle more obtuse after eight weeks of physical therapy at rest (p=0.022) and at contraction (p=.007), more obtuse anorectal angle at valsalva (p=.013), and a larger antero-posterior diameter at rest (p=.025) than controls. This is the first study to objectively demonstrate changes of the PFM of women with vulvodynia after being submitted to treatment with and without physical therapy. Kinesiotherapy was efficient on promoting significant modification of the PFM biometry in this population. These findings adds contribution for further understanding of mechanism of action of physical therapy treatment of women with vulvodynia Ver menos
